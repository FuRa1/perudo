Perudo mobile — the whole flow, one board
Every mobile state a player can reach — arrival, the round, every way a round ends, palifico, a dropped connection, a refused tap, twelve cups — in the lantern-cabin language: one dark cabin ground, one warm pool of light, cream dice loudest. 390×844, holding at 320. One bid sheet, one action area, one primary action per screen, and exactly one surface per event.
This file is the basis for the Perudo design: where it disagrees with anything else under /designs, it wins. Build from it without opening another file — and take values from §0 Tokens rather than reading them out of this markup. Nothing on this page uses a colour, radius, shadow or type size that isn't published there.

Surfaces
Four, and only four: the dark table, the light card, the on-table translucent fill, the scrim.
cabin ground — #5a462d → #33281c → #211a13
One gradient, one recipe, every screen: radial-gradient(112% 58% at 50% 19%, …). The lamp is at 19% from the top, so the light always falls from above and behind the table. Stops move only for the lobby (46% / 12%, a taller cabin because there is no arc).
sheet — #2b231b
The one opaque dark surface that sits above the table: the fixed bid sheet, and the connection-lost card. Flat, never gradient — that is how you tell “furniture” from “table”.
card cream — #f5ead8
A lifted patch of paper on the table. Used for exactly three things: the room-code card, the wager token, the reveal / outcome recap. If something is on card cream, it is the thing the player is meant to read right now. Never a full-screen background on mobile.
on-table fill — rgba(242,231,211,.05 / .06 / .07 / .09 / .11)
Cream at low alpha, the only way a container is drawn on the cabin without lifting off it. Read the alpha as loudness: .05 another player's roster row, .06–.07 a field or sub-panel inside the sheet, .09 a chip or stepper, .11 a row that belongs to you. Borders are the same cream at .10–.20. Cream at alpha is always a fill or a border — never text.
scrim — rgba(20,14,8,.62)
Warm-black, not neutral black. Two uses only: behind the connection-lost card, and dimming the table under the reveal recap. One scrim on screen at a time — if you need a second, the first surface was wrong.
paper — #fffdf7 not in the app
This document's own annotation cards, plus the #e7e0d3 page behind them. Neither ships. Do not implement either.
Identity is not the same as role
The wood browns draw an object. Terracotta marks you, and marks the action. They are never interchangeable.
cup wood — linear-gradient(180deg,#6b4f31,#33261a)
The material an opponent's overturned cup is made of. It is scenery, not a brand colour and not a player colour: every cup on the table is this same wood, because a cup is a cup. Rim highlight rgba(255,224,176,.20). Never use it for a badge, a button, or text — on the cabin it is nearly invisible, which is exactly how a closed cup should read.
cup wood, lit — linear-gradient(180deg,#8a663d,#3d2d1c)
The same cup with the lamp on it — the active bidder's seat, and nothing else. Rim goes to .34. Deliberately near-identical to the unlit cup: the seat halo does the shouting, the wood only agrees with it.
terracotta — #c67139
Three jobs, one meaning — this is yours, or this is the move: (1) the single primary button on any screen, (2) the local player's lobby avatar disc, (3) the 2px ring on a reveal die that counted toward the claim. Opponent avatars are never terracotta and never coloured per player — they are translucent cream. One terracotta fill per screen; if you want a second action, it is outlined.
0:07
brass — #f0c98d
Attention on the dark ground, always as ink or a hairline — never a fill: the live timer numerals, “it is your turn” captions, the HOST pill, the text caret, the BIDDING label, the palifico chip's edge. If brass appears, the player is being asked to look. Borders use it at .34–.60 alpha.
lamp core — #f7d69b one-off
Appears once in the whole product: the lit disc of the brand mark on the nickname screen, as radial-gradient(60% 60% at 50% 32%,#f7d69b,#c67139) with a 0 0 46px rgba(247,214,155,.42) bloom. It is the lamp. Do not reuse it as an accent — that is what brass is for.

Radius
Six steps. Each one means a kind of thing, so a reader can tell what something is from its corner.
999px — every button, chip, pill and text field. If it is tappable and not a die, it is a pill.
28px top-only — the bid sheet, the one surface that slides up from the bottom edge.
26px / 24px — a lifted card. 26 on the table (reveal, wager, outcome), 24 in the lobby.
20px / 18px — a row or a sub-panel inside something else: roster rows, seat halo, code tiles, sheet fields.
13 / 12 / 11 / 9 / 8px — a die, scaled with its own size: 58px face → 13, 50 → 12, 44 → 11, 34 → 9, 30 → 8. Keep the ratio near 0.23× the face; the 88px public die is the exception at 20px.
44px — the phone bezel in this document. Documentation only; the app has no such corner.
Dice padding. A die's pip grid uses padding ≈ 0.16× the face and a pip ≈ 0.19× the face — 44px face, 7px padding, 8px pip. Larger padding is what produced the dead-space dice in the build; the pips should almost touch the corners.

Shadow — elevation is meaning
How far off the table something sits says how much it matters. Seven values, each owned.
0 16px 40px rgba(0,0,0,.44) — the reveal / outcome recap. The highest thing on the table.
0 -16px 34px rgba(0,0,0,.42) — the bid sheet, cast upward; the only negative-Y shadow.
0 14px 34px rgba(0,0,0,.46) — the roll cup. Heavy because it is a physical object.
0 12px 30px rgba(0,0,0,.34) — the room-code card in the lobby.
0 10px 26px rgba(0,0,0,.40) — the wager token (8px 22px .36 in its compact form).
0 3px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.34) — one die in your hand strip. Barely lifted, but not flat.
0 8px 26px rgba(0,0,0,.44), 0 0 40px rgba(247,214,155,.20) — the 88px public opening die: a shadow and a lamp bloom, because it is the one die the whole table is looking at.
0 6px 18px rgba(198,113,57,.44) — under the primary button only.
0 24px 60px rgba(46,43,37,.34) and 0 3px 10px rgba(46,43,37,.07) are this document's phone bezel and annotation cards. Not app values.
Geometry and touch
56px — primary and destructive actions, full width or in a 1.4 / 1 pair.
48px — the lobby's share row (button + 48px round icon).
44–50px — a die in the hand strip; 50 at 2–8 players, 44 when the sheet is open.
36px — header chips. Not tappable, so below the 44 floor on purpose.
Screen padding 24px on arrival screens, 20px on the table and lobby, 26px for the status bar's own inset.
Status bar 54px; the bid sheet's bottom padding carries the home indicator at 26–34px.
Gaps follow 5 / 6 / 7 / 8 / 10 / 12 / 14 / 16px. Anything larger is margin-top:auto doing the work instead.
Two things to fix, not to copy. The room-code screen's back circle is drawn 42px — below the 44 floor; build it at 44. And 320px: at that width the five code tiles and the five-die hand strip are the tightest rows in the product. Both are flex-sized rather than fixed, so they compress rather than scroll — hold the gap and let the tile shrink, never the reverse.

3 · Round outcomes
one event, one surface — the recap card carries every loss
Decision: RoundLossModal is retired on mobile
The reveal card is the loss surface. It already carries strictly more than the modal does — every hand, the ringed counted dice, the verdict and the closing summary, where the modal shows only claimed vs actual — and it needs no dismissal before the player can move on. Two surfaces narrating one event also means two shadows, two scrims and a tap that buys nothing.

The modal's one unique job was the “Time ran out” case, which has no reveal behind it. Panel 10 below gives that its own card in the same shape, so nothing is lost by removing the modal from the mobile board. Keep RoundLossModal mounted for desktop; on mobile, don't present it.

The five refusals, and where each one lands
IN SHEET
“Too low — beat 4 × fives or call” · illegal bid. Replaces the hint row, disables Place bid. Never a toast — the fix is two inches below it.
IN SHEET
“Face is locked this round” · wrong phase. Same slot, shown if a locked stepper is pressed.
TOAST
“Anne got there first” · not your turn. A late tap that the server refused — the sheet has already collapsed, so there is no field to attach to.
ON ENTRY
“That table is full” / “No table with that code” · room full, bad code. Sits above the Join button in #f0b393, and the fifth tile keeps focus.
ON ENTRY
“This seat has expired — take a new one” · invalid token. Terminal: clears the stored session and returns to the nickname step.
TO BUILDRule 4.2 — refusal without a toast
Two rules. If the player can fix it here, say it here: the message takes the hint row's slot (same height, so nothing moves), the offending number turns #f0b393, and Place bid drops to its disabled 32% fill. Only use a toast when the surface that caused it is gone. Toasts sit under the status bar, use the sheet surface, and last 3s. No error ever announces a state name — “wrong phase” becomes “Face is locked this round”, and there is no such thing as an error dialog in this product.

NEEDS PASSEleven opponents, drawn in this file's language
Above 8 opponents the absolute arc gives way to two flex rows. The back row must read as further away, not merely smaller: it is at opacity:.6, its cups are 26×19 against the front row's 30×22, its pips drop to 4px, and it loses the row padding — four cues in the same direction, because size alone reads as a rendering bug. Seats are flex:1;min-width:0 with truncating names, so eleven cups and your own hand hold at 320px without sideways scroll. The wager token drops a step (30px numeral, 34px die, 18px radius) and the hint chips are dropped entirely — at this scale the legal-raise hint is longer than the screen is wide. Nothing about the sheet or the hand strip changes; the arc absorbs the whole difference.

Turn timer — the three phases of rule 5.6
One control, four appearances. Always mm:ss counting down — the app's bare 0s hides which phase you are in.
0:25
Quiet · 0–15s. Outline only, no fill, no pulse, silent. The number is present but not asking for anything.
0:07
Warning · 15–25s. Gains the terracotta wash, brass ink and a 1s pulse, and the audible tick starts. This is the state panel 07 draws.
0:22 bank
Bank draining · the 30s reserve. The word bank joins the numerals, the edge steps to the challenge outline, and a hairline drain bar appears under the chip — the only progress bar on the table. It is the one phase where a number going down costs the player something, so it gets a second channel.
0:00
Spent. Stops pulsing, drops to quiet grey, stays on screen through panel 10 so the cause of the loss is still legible after the fact.
Sits opposite the phase chip in the header, and only ever describes your clock: while another player is deciding it shows their remaining time in the quiet style and never pulses, beeps or banks — a chip shouting at you about someone else's turn is noise. Both durations are config (TIMING_CONFIG), so treat 25/30 as the current values, not as constants baked into the design.
